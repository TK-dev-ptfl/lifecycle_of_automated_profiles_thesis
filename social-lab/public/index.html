How do people use a social feed?

A research study for a master's thesis at (institution). It takes about 5–8 minutes.

What you will do

  1. Browse a made-up social network called Hearth for about a minute and a half, the way you normally would.
  2. Do six small things: like a few posts, write a comment, write a post, open a profile, follow someone.
  3. Answer seven short multiple-choice questions.

Everything on Hearth is invented. Nothing you do is seen by anyone else or posted anywhere.

Why

We are studying how people move the mouse, type, scroll and read on social media, so that automated accounts (bots) can be told apart from people. Your session is an example of how a real person behaves.

What is recorded

  • How you use the mouse, keyboard and touch screen: pointer positions and times, clicks, scrolling, and the timing of key presses. Not which keys: only the kind of key (letter, space, backspace, …).
  • What you do on Hearth: which posts were on screen and for how long, likes, follows, and the length of anything you write. The text of your comments and posts is not saved.
  • Technical details of your browser and device: browser and version, operating system, screen size, graphics card name, language, time zone, number of processor cores, and similar settings.
  • Technical details of your connection: how your browser sets up the secure connection, the order of its request headers, and the country and type of network your address belongs to (e.g. home broadband, mobile, data centre). Your IP address is not stored.
  • This site uses Google reCAPTCHA v3, which sends information about your visit to Google. Google's Privacy Policy and Terms apply.
  • One connectivity check contacts a Google STUN server. Its result is stored only as yes/no.
  • Your answers to the questions at the end.

We do not collect your name, email, account details, or anything that is shown on other websites.

Your rights

  • Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time with the Stop and delete button, and everything recorded in your session is deleted at once.
  • At the end you get a deletion code. With it you can delete your data later, at the bottom of this page.
  • Your session is encrypted on the study server as soon as you finish and then stored in a private repository on GitHub (GitHub, Inc., USA). Only the researcher holds the key that can read it; GitHub cannot. Deleting with your code removes it at once; encrypted copies in the repository's version history are purged within 30 days.
  • Your session is stored only on the researcher's own storage.
  • Data is kept until the thesis is defended and the research is complete (at most 3 years), and is used only for this research. Results are published only as statistics.
  • Questions or requests: the researcher, (contact).

What this was about

Bots on social networks increasingly imitate people. The usual tells (perfectly straight mouse movements, identical pauses, typing with no rhythm, reading posts faster than anyone can read) only work if we know what real people do. Your session adds one real example: how long you looked at posts of different lengths, how your pointer accelerated and slowed down near a button, how long you held each key, how you scrolled. Together with sessions from other people it shows the range of normal behaviour, which is what both detectors and the study's own measurements are checked against.
